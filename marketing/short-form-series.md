# EarlyOtter Short-Form Video Series

## Recommended campaign

**Series name:** *Otis & Olive vs. Tomorrow*

**Campaign line:** **Life changes. Your alarm should too.**

**Brand sign-off:** **Wake up to what matters. EarlyOtter.**

The series follows Otis and his girlfriend Olive through a week of changing classes, work calls, commutes, workouts, trips, and days off. Their relationship is the emotional thread; the comedy comes from a normal alarm treating every day as if it were the same.

The story should feel connected without requiring viewers to watch in order. Every episode must still work as someone's first encounter with EarlyOtter.

## Core story

Otis and Olive are lovable, capable, and busy—but their mornings are chaos because tomorrow never looks like yesterday. At first, they are always reacting: traffic, late arrivals, rushed coffee, and half-awake calls. Then EarlyOtter quietly becomes part of their routine. It reads the next calendar event and accounts for prep and commute time, so the couple spends less time fighting the morning and more time enjoying their life together.

The app is not the hero that magically fixes everything. Otis and Olive are the heroes; EarlyOtter gives them the right start.

## Characters and visual language

- **Otis:** Earnest, confident, slightly overcommitted. He is the otter in the existing boxing video.
- **Olive:** Clever, expressive, and equally central—not just the girlfriend reacting to Otis. Class, exams, presentations, and trips give her independent stories.
- **The antagonist:** “Tomorrow”—represented by calendar changes, traffic, early meetings, and the glowing time on a phone.
- **Recurring cues:** warm orange light for EarlyOtter, blue calendar-event cards, one smooth pebble hidden in each scene, and the same two-note alarm sound.
- **Look:** cinematic photoreal otters in ordinary human spaces. Keep wardrobe and facial markings consistent between generations.

## Repeatable 10–20 second structure

1. **0–2s — Hook:** Open on the consequence, not an establishing shot.
2. **2–8s — Escalation:** One readable problem and one strong reaction.
3. **8–13s — Turn or punchline:** Reveal the calendar context or relationship beat.
4. **13–17s — Product proof:** Show the event, prep, commute, and calculated alarm in one clean visual.
5. **Final 1–2s — Sign-off:** “Life changes. Your alarm should too.” / EarlyOtter logo.

Use little or no dialogue. Facial acting, sound design, and 3–7 words of on-screen text will travel better across platforms.

## Season one posting order

### 1. The Commute

**Purpose:** Lead with the most universally understandable pain.

Otis is stopped in traffic, gripping the wheel while Olive silently holds up a phone: “Team sync — 8:30.” The clock changes to 8:29. Otis gives the camera a tiny, doomed look. Do not depict reckless driving; the comedy is the stationary traffic and mounting panic.

**Product beat:** Flash the corrected version: EarlyOtter subtracts both 45 minutes of prep and 30 minutes of commute from the 8:30 event.

**End text:** “His alarm knew 7:30. It didn't know about the commute.”

### 2. Olive's 9 AM

**Purpose:** Introduce Olive as a co-lead and immediately widen the audience to students.

Open on a lecture-hall door closing. Olive arrives one beat late with backpack, coffee, and papers sliding everywhere. Cut to her calendar event: “Biology Class — 9:00” and a generic 8:00 alarm.

**Product beat:** Replay the crucial moment with an EarlyOtter alarm at 7:55, calculated from her class rule.

**End text:** “A class alarm should know about getting ready.”

### 3. Same Alarm, Different Day

**Purpose:** Give the clearest explanation of the product.

Fast split screen: Monday has a 9:00 class, Tuesday an 11:00 call, Wednesday an 8:00 presentation—but the old alarm says 7:30 every day. Replace it with EarlyOtter calculating three different wake times from the calendar.

**End text:** “Life changes. Your alarm should too.”

### 4. Fighting Tomorrow

**Purpose:** Publish the existing boxing asset after the audience understands the premise.

Frame the boxing footage as Otis training for an unexpectedly early morning. Start with “POV: tomorrow's meeting moved to 7 AM.” His sparring opponent can be labeled “Tomorrow.” Finish on the EarlyOtter alarm automatically moving earlier.

**End text:** “Ready for the early round.”

### 5. Camera On

**Purpose:** Deliver a highly relatable work-from-home episode.

Otis sits glassy-eyed in a dull video call. A voice says, “Otis, want to take this one?” His eyes widen; he is muted and still wearing pajama bottoms. Olive crosses behind him, sees the scene, and quietly backs away.

**Product beat:** Tomorrow's call gets its own prep rule instead of using the commute rule.

### 6. The Exam

**Purpose:** Give Olive a win and show that different events can use different preparation times.

Olive wakes calmly, reviews one final flash card, and walks into an exam as another student sprints past in panic. Otis waits outside with two coffees and an encouraging sign held upside down.

**End text:** “More prep for the mornings that need it.”

### 7. Airport Math

**Purpose:** Show an extreme, high-value use case.

Rapid visual equation: 6:15 flight + packing + drive + security = a brutally early alarm. Otis stares at the calculated time, then slowly puts on his boxing gloves from episode four. Olive gently removes them and hands him coffee.

**End text:** “Early? Yes. Surprised? No.”

### 8. The Presentation

**Purpose:** Complete Otis's redemption arc.

Mirror episode one: a clock approaches meeting time, but Otis is already composed in the conference room. He starts the presentation, then realizes one tiny whisker is stuck to his slide remote. Olive sends a supportive text.

**End text:** “On time looks good on you.”

### 9. First in Line

**Purpose:** Deliver the amusement-park payoff and show that “what matters” is not only work.

Otis and Olive stand alone at the entrance, then smash-cut to them laughing on a ride. A late crowd floods in behind them. The calendar event was “Adventure Day,” with a longer commute rule.

**End text:** “Wake up to what matters.”

### 10. Nothing Tomorrow

**Purpose:** End the first arc warmly and show that the app does not force every day to look the same.

Morning light fills the room. No alarm sounds. Otis wakes, checks a calendar with no early event, smiles, and curls back up beside Olive. A final shot shows breakfast by the water later that morning.

**End text:** “Sometimes the right alarm is no alarm.”

## Additional episode bank

- **Dentist:** Otis confidently arrives early—at the wrong branch. Use location-based commute time as the setup for a future feature or avoid a product claim if location-aware routing is not supported.
- **Interview Outfit:** Olive's event needs 60 minutes of prep; Otis tries to help and creates an absurd pile of rejected outfits.
- **Daylight Saving:** Every clock in the apartment disagrees; EarlyOtter is the calm object in the room.
- **The Concert Aftermath:** They get home late, and the next morning's schedule starts later—no unnecessary punishment alarm.
- **Laundry Call:** Otis joins a work call polished from the waist up while searching for one missing sock below frame.
- **Rainy Commute:** They are ready on time, but one umbrella is sized for a human and swallows both otters.
- **Anniversary Breakfast:** Olive assumes Otis forgot; the calendar event was actually used to wake him early enough to cook.
- **The Snooze Villain:** A recurring black-gloved paw reaches for Snooze; Otis blocks it with his boxing technique.

## Publishing and creative rules

- Post the first four in the order above; after that, episodes can be reordered based on which audience responds best.
- Start with three posts per week for four weeks. Alternate stress, competence, and warmth so the feed does not become twelve variations of “running late.”
- Keep the same Otis model from the boxing output, but create a clearly distinct and consistent Olive reference before generating her episodes.
- Treat the boxing clip as character development, not the overall visual theme. The contrast between a tiny otter and very serious training is what makes it memorable.
- Show the product's actual behavior: the next calendar event plus chosen prep and commute time produces the alarm. Avoid implying live traffic routing, guaranteed background updates, or features not currently shipped.
- Use a clean 9:16 master, large captions inside safe areas, and a strong visual change in the first second.
- Keep the app UI legible for only one idea per video. A viewer should understand the product without pausing.

## What to test

For the first four episodes, keep the video the same and test two opening captions:

- **Relatable:** “When tomorrow starts earlier than you remembered.”
- **Direct:** “Your calendar should set your alarm.”

Judge the concept primarily on three signals: three-second hold, completion rate, and profile/App Store taps. Views alone can reward a funny otter without proving that viewers understood EarlyOtter.
